Consecutive stat-heavy slides are the primary audience-attrition risk in a talk.
Around the third one, attention drifts — and this holds even when the *sequence*
is well-built.

**Why:** From the 2026-04-15 express deck review. Slides 2–6 presented five
consecutive stat screens. The arc was genuinely sound
(positive → nuanced → contradicted → emotional pivot), and it still carried a
real risk: structural soundness does not rescue density, it only means the
speaker *can* rescue it with strong pacing. If read verbatim, the audience is
gone by slide three.

**How to apply:** Count consecutive data slides during review and flag runs of
three or more. The fix is a pattern break — an anecdote, an image, a question —
not tighter numbers. Do not accept "but the narrative arc works" as a
resolution; the arc and the density are separate findings. In the reviewed deck
the pattern break that worked was a first-person anecdote, which was also the
strongest slide in the talk.
